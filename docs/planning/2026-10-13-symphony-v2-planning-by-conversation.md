# Symphony v2: planning by conversation

2026-10-13. Agreed with Scott after we rebuilt the household's routines in one
conversation. Comes after wall v1, which is the first place it is proven.

## Why

The routine data had drifted from real life because changing it meant
operating editing machinery: forms, wizards, step screens, a "tend" drawer.
One conversation fixed weekdays and weekends in under an hour. Talk in,
picture out.

## The shape of Symphony v2

Four building blocks, and little else:

1. **The picture.** Today, the week, the wall. Mostly for looking; drawn
   graphically; shows what is true.
2. **The conversation.** The docked bar (and the wall's voice). The main
   way to change things.
3. **The change card.** Symphony's proposal ("these merge, these move,
   these go"), one Approve, and Undo after. This is what makes talking
   trustworthy.
4. **The item panel.** Notes, people, links and places for one thing.

One-item edits stay direct (tick it done, drag it to tomorrow). Talk is for
changes that touch several things. The picture must work for someone who
never talks to it.

## The weekly planning loop (about 10 minutes)

1. **Symphony prepares.** It reads last week (what was done, what wasn't,
   what happened), the coming week (calendar, routines, school notices), and
   glances at the month, season and year as reference.
2. **It asks only what is unresolved.** Never the same questionnaire:
   "Three things from last week didn't happen: carry, drop, or someday?" ·
   "Baseball and the dentist are both Sunday at 10:30." · "Nothing this week
   serves *Plan sabbatical*; anything, or leave it?" (only when relevant,
   never pushing).
3. **It checks its other sources before asking or creating.** The calendar
   (Grampappa on Fridays), school email and ClassDojo, existing routines. No
   duplicates.
4. **One change list, one yes.** Symphony backs up first, applies
   everything at once, and verifies.
5. **The picture rebuilds.** The week and the wall show what is now true.

The higher levels run on their own cadence: a short month check when a
month starts, a season check each season, the year once. Weekly sessions
only glance at them.

## Rules that carry over

- **Structures, never supplies.** Everything comes from the family's words
  and data; Symphony organises it.
- **Two layers.** The household week is shared and leads at the wall; each
  person's goals stay private to them.
- **Rules decide, Symphony assists.** What leads on a screen is decided by
  time, what's next and what's unfinished, not by the model.
- **Keep it right.** When an event contradicts a routine, Symphony asks
  once ("Is pickup 4:30 on Thursdays now?") instead of growing a cleanup
  list.

## Order of work

1. Wall v1 (picture, voice, change card) in the household layer.
2. Plan the week rebuilt as this loop, on desktop and at the wall.
3. Today and Week trimmed to the four blocks; editing machinery retired.
