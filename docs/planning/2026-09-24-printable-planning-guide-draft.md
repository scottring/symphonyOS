# Printable planning guide — content draft

**Status: review-only draft. Not built, not designed, not app code.** This is the
wording and the shape of the paper, for Codex to review before anything is laid out
or implemented.

Written to Scott's correction of 2026-09-24: **clarity, efficiency, accurate
interpretation, fewer steps.** Not enjoyment. The guide should get someone from a
blank page to a short, honest list and no further. Every exercise is optional and
every page stands alone.

---

## Sources, and what is original here

Scott asked for Sarah Hart-Unger's *Best Laid Plans* as a reference. Two official
show-notes pages were inspected:

- [Episode 218 — Your New Month Guide](https://theshubox.com/2024/09/episode-218-your-new-month-guide.html)
- [Episode 317 — Reasons to Plan Seasonally](https://theshubox.com/2026/08/ep-317-reasons-to-plan-seasonally-goal-planners-farewell-to-powersheets-amplify.html)

**What those pages actually support, and nothing more:**

1. A named **"Nested Goals"** framework — the idea that an individual month sits
   inside a larger goal structure. The show notes name it; they do not describe its
   mechanics.
2. A **monthly ritual** practised regularly, and a **quarterly (seasonal) planning
   session** the host reports having benefited from. The notes say she does these;
   they do not set out how.
3. The host advocates **planning in enjoyment at the monthly level.**

**The book has not been read.** Neither episode has been listened to. The show notes
are episode announcements, not method documents, and they do not contain the prompts,
steps, or worksheets. Nothing below should be described to anyone as *her* method. No
worksheet, printable, or planner page of hers is reproduced or adapted here.

**Where this draft deliberately diverges:** point 3. Scott's instruction is the
opposite — do not emphasise fun or enjoyable activities. This guide optimises for
clarity, efficiency, accurate interpretation and fewer steps, and has no "plan in
fun" exercise.

**Everything else below is original to Symphony**, and derives from Symphony's own
model as the walkthrough has now tested it: goals describe outcomes and tasks
describe actions; periods have explicit dates; routines are patterns and occurrences
are commitments; planning a task for today preserves its broader commitments; the
cascade is available but never mandatory.

## Shape of the paper

Four sheets, each usable alone:

| Sheet | For | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Week** | one person, or a household at a kitchen table | 10 min |
| **Month** | the same, once | 15 min |
| **Season** | usually a household, four times a year | 25 min |
| **Year** | usually a household, once | 30 min |

**None is a prerequisite for another.** A person who only ever fills in the Week sheet
is using the guide correctly. The Year sheet says so in as many words.

Every sheet carries the same five bands in the same order, so the second sheet someone
picks up is already familiar:

1. **This is** — the period and year, written by hand
2. **What happened** — a brief look-back
3. **What is already committed** — existing obligations and capacity
4. **What matters here** — the chosen priorities, deliberately few
5. **What that means** — next actions, routines, events, ideas

Bands 2–5 are each skippable and say so.

---

## Band 1 — This is

> **This sheet is for:** ______________________ (week of / month of / season of / year)
> **Year:** __________  **Written by:** ______________________  **Date filled in:** __________

Three lines, top of every sheet, same place.

**Why it is first and why it is mandatory:** this is the one field the scanner cannot
guess and must not invent. A sheet without a period is a sheet whose every item lands
in the wrong place, or in no place. The Week sheet asks for the week's **start date**
in digits, not "next week" — a relative phrase on paper is ambiguous by the time it is
scanned.

*Original to Symphony: Symphony's periods carry explicit dates, so the paper has to
as well.*

---

## Band 2 — What happened

Short, factual, and explicitly skippable. Three prompts, no more:

> **Done, and worth recording:**
> _______________________________________________
>
> **Carried, and still wanted:**
> _______________________________________________
>
> **Let go:**
> _______________________________________________

No scoring, no ratings, no "how did that feel". The look-back exists to make the
carry-forward decision deliberate — which is Symphony's own review model — not to
produce a reflection.

On the Season and Year sheets a fourth line is added:

> **What I now know that I didn't at the start:**

*Original to Symphony. The three verdicts map to what a Symphony review already
offers — Done, Keep, Drop — so a scanned look-back can be read without interpretation.*

---

## Band 3 — What is already committed

Before choosing anything new, write down what the period already holds. Two columns:

> **Already on the calendar** (fixed dates, other people's dates)
> ____________________  ____________________  ____________________
>
> **Already repeating** (things that happen anyway)
> ____________________  ____________________  ____________________

Then one capacity line, in the person's own units:

> **Realistically, the time I have for anything new this period is:**
> ____________________________________

Deliberately not a number of hours. "Two evenings", "one Saturday", "almost none" are
all valid and all more accurate than a figure someone invents to fill a box.

**Household variant.** The Season and Year sheets give each person a short column of
their own and one shared column. The shared column is for what the household holds
together; the personal columns are for what only that person holds. Nothing is totalled
and nothing is compared.

*Original to Symphony. This band exists because the walkthrough found the opposite
failure in the app — a planning session describing a month as empty when it already
held a goal and three tasks (S3-04). On paper, capacity comes before choosing, so the
choice is made against what is already there.*

---

## Band 4 — What matters here

> **By the end of this period, what do you want to be true?**
>
> 1. _______________________________________________
> 2. _______________________________________________
> 3. _______________________________________________

Three numbered lines on the Month, Season and Year sheets; **two** on the Week sheet.
The count is the constraint — the lines run out, which is the point.

Under them, one line:

> **If only one of these happens, which one?**  ______

**Wording discipline.** The prompt asks what should be *true*, not what to *do*. That
is the outcome/action distinction Symphony rests on, asked in plain language, without
ever using the words "goal" or "outcome" on the paper.

**Optional, and only where it is real** — one line beneath each priority:

> *serves:* _______________________________________________

A month priority may name a season priority; a season priority may name a year
priority. **Left blank is the normal case, not an omission.** The guide says so
directly, because a blank line here is honest and a guessed one is not.

*Original to Symphony. It is the paper counterpart of the goal-supports-goal
relationship verified live on 2026-09-24. It is also where Symphony most resembles the
"Nested Goals" idea named in the Episode 218 notes — but the notes do not describe that
framework, so this line is Symphony's own construction, arrived at from Symphony's
data model, not an adaptation of hers.*

## Band 5 — What that means

The most error-prone part of the whole guide, because this is where a scanner has to
classify handwriting. The layout does the classifying, so the reader never has to learn
a vocabulary and the scanner never has to guess from wording.

**Four labelled areas, on the sheet, in this order:**

> **Next actions** — one thing you could sit down and do
> ☐ ____________________________________  *who:* ______  *when:* ______
> ☐ ____________________________________  *who:* ______  *when:* ______
> ☐ ____________________________________  *who:* ______  *when:* ______
>
> **Happens repeatedly** — a pattern, not a single occasion
> ↻ ____________________________________  *how often:* ______  *who:* ______
> ↻ ____________________________________  *how often:* ______  *who:* ______
>
> **Fixed date and time** — already set, or set by someone else
> ▣ ____________________________________  *date:* ______  *time:* ______
> ▣ ____________________________________  *date:* ______  *time:* ______
>
> **Not yet decided** — worth keeping, not committed to
> ○ ____________________________________
> ○ ____________________________________
> ○ ____________________________________

**The rules this band follows, and why:**

- **Position carries the classification, not wording.** Which box a line is written in
  is the signal. A scanner reading "swim lessons" cannot tell a routine from an event
  from an idea; the box it sits in can.
- **The glyphs are printed, not drawn.** ☐ ↻ ▣ ○ are pre-printed so a scan has a second,
  redundant signal when handwriting crosses a boundary line.
- **`who` and `when` only when known.** Both are explicitly optional on the sheet, with
  a printed note: *leave blank if you do not know yet — a blank is kept as a blank.*
  This is the whole reason the fields are small: a large field invites invention.
- **"Not yet decided" is the largest area** and comes last. A brainstorm must have
  somewhere to go that is not a commitment. The guide states in print that nothing
  written here becomes a commitment when scanned.
- **Next actions do not require a date.** The sheet says so. This is the paper
  counterpart of the app behaviour the mockup demonstrates: an action can exist under a
  goal with no timing, and get its timing later.

*Original to Symphony. The four categories are Symphony's own item kinds — task,
routine, event, and unprocessed capture — expressed without the product's vocabulary.*

## A free brainstorm comes first, and is not a sheet

Before Band 4 on the Season and Year sheets, one unruled half-page:

> **Anything, in any order. Nothing here is a commitment.**

It is unruled on purpose. Ruling it would make it a list, and a list reads as a set of
intentions. The printed note under it says that everything on this half-page is treated
as "not yet decided" unless it is copied into a band above.

## How the paper and Plan-from-paper are designed together

Not a claim about the current importer. These are requirements for the review surface,
to be designed with the paper, and they are what the mockup's sample review screen
illustrates.

1. **The original is kept and shown.** The scan sits beside the proposal, always, so
   the reader checks the interpretation against their own handwriting rather than
   trusting it.
2. **Every proposed item shows its classification, and the classification is editable
   in one control** — next action / repeats / fixed date / not yet decided. Changing it
   does not re-run anything and does not lose the other fields.
3. **Every proposed relationship is shown and editable** — which priority an action
   serves, which period it belongs to. Shown as a named link, not an id.
4. **Uncertainty is marked, not resolved.** Unclear handwriting, an ambiguous date, an
   item that could be two things: flagged in place, with the transcription the reader
   can correct. An uncertain field is never quietly filled with a best guess.
5. **Blank stays blank.** No owner, no date, no period is invented. If the sheet has no
   week, the item arrives with no week.
6. **Nothing is created until the reader approves it**, and the approval is per item,
   not one button for the page.
7. **A place for instructions and dialogue** beside the proposal, where the reader can
   say what the page means — "these are all Mia's", "the second column is November" —
   and see the proposal change in response, before approving.
8. **Existing data is checked before creating.** A proposal that matches something
   already in Symphony says so and offers to attach rather than duplicate.

*Original to Symphony, and consistent with the product principle that reviews make
carry-forward decisions deliberate.*

## Open questions for Codex

1. **Sheet count.** Four sheets, or one folded sheet per period with Week on the
   outside? Four is simpler to print; one fold is fewer pieces of paper to lose.
2. **Household columns.** Per-person columns on Season and Year only, as drafted, or on
   Month too? Month is where a household most often collides, but the sheet is already
   the densest of the four.
3. **The `serves:` line.** Drafted as free text under each priority. It could instead be
   a short code the reader copies from the sheet above (e.g. "S2" for the season's
   second priority), which scans far more reliably but asks the reader to learn a
   convention. Reliability against fewer steps.
4. **Whether the Week sheet gets Band 3 at all.** Capacity over one week may be better
   expressed as the week's fixed dates alone, dropping the capacity line and saving a
   third of the sheet.
5. **Print target.** US Letter assumed. Confirm before layout — it changes the band
   heights, not the content.
