# A year in the life of a task, an event, a routine and a note

Written 2026-09-20 against branch `claude/item-pathways` (main `46aa9280`
plus six commits). Plain language, no column names. The technical map is
`docs/item-pathways.md`; this is the story it tells. Two things described
here, the one shared week list and the weekend window, exist only on that
branch and are not deployed.

## A task

**Birth.** A task is born the moment you say something needs doing. Most of
them arrive with nothing but a title and land in the Inbox: a line typed into
⌘K, a capture from the phone, the Mac capture window, a photo, a Supernote
page, a WhatsApp message, a to-do pulled out of a school email. A few are born
already placed. Type it on Today and it is dated today and chosen for today.
Add it on the month or season page and it joins that list. Snap a paper plan
and every line lands at the altitude the page was written for: a date, this
week, this month, this season, or a year goal.

**Sorting.** A task also arrives with or without a life area. A capture with
no life area is Unsorted, and the app will not let you place it anywhere
until you answer "Where does this belong?" Work and Personal stay private.
Family is shared with the household. Choosing a day is deliberately exempt
from that gate, because urgent things skip the cascade.

**The Inbox.** In the Inbox you give it a verdict: today, tomorrow, this week,
a date, someday, done, or gone. That verdict is the only thing that moves it
out. Nothing ages out of the Inbox on its own, but after two weeks it starts
appearing in Today's "need a decision" count.

**The ladder.** From here a task lives on exactly one rung: someday, a season,
a month, a week, or a day. Moving up or sideways is a real move. Moving down
from a month or a season is a copy. The original stays on its list marked
"→ placed", and later "→ done", so a month list is also the record of what
became of it. Only one open copy of a thing can be in flight at a time. A
goal is the exception on every rung: it is ticked, never placed, and the
steps that serve it hang underneath it.

**The week.** On the week list a task waits to be given a day. There is one
week list, and the Today pin's "This week" group and the column on `/week`
are the same list. Drag it onto a day and it is dated. Choose it for today
instead and it keeps its place on the week list and also appears in Today's
"My focus", in the order you set. Un-choosing removes it from focus and
nothing else.

**The day.** On its day it is a commitment. Timed work sits in "Still ahead"
until its time passes, then rolls into "Earlier today". Untimed work you
merely dated waits in the Today pin as "Scheduled today" until you choose it.
Tick it and it is done. Nothing records when. A finished task stays on the
list it belonged to, struck through, because the list is the record.

**When the day passes and it wasn't done.** The date is never rewritten. That
one decision shapes everything after. For two days Today still shows it as
carried over. The morning after its day, `/week` returns it to the top of the
week list with "Didn't happen · Thursday", and the grid keeps drawing it on
Thursday, faded and dashed. On the third day it slips off Today entirely and
joins the "need a decision" count. Push it deliberately and the push is
counted; let it slide and only its age tells the story. The complete list of
everything past its date, uncapped, is the Expired fold at the bottom of the
Inbox. Nothing is ever lost, and nothing quietly moves itself.

**When the month or season ends.** The period page shows a look-back. For each
open row you choose: done, keep (a fresh copy on the next period), someday,
or drop. Done rows and placed-and-done rows are the win column. A kept goal
carries its open steps with it. A year goal is a different creature, kept in
its own table with its own page, and keeping it means a new goal for the new
year.

**Someday.** A task sent to Someday is out of every list and every count, on
purpose. Right now the Someday page redirects to Today, so the only ways back
are search and the assistant. It is a one-way door until that page exists
again.

## An event

**Birth.** An event is born in Google Calendar, not in Symphony. Type one into
⌘K with a calendar connected and Symphony writes it to Google. Add a
recurrence and it becomes a Google repeating rule. The school-email reader is
the one path that makes an event-shaped row in Symphony's own table, dated
and timed, for a day that never reached the calendar.

**Where it shows.** Google is the truth and Symphony reads it for the days on
screen, re-reading when you come back to the tab. There is no live feed and no
polling, so an event added elsewhere appears when you return. On Today, timed
events sit in "Still ahead"; all-day events are listed above it. An event is
never in "My focus" and never in the Today pin, because an event is time, not
a decision. On the kitchen wall it lands in a person's lane by the first name
in its title. Meal-plan dinners are dressed as events and ride alongside.

**What Symphony adds.** Symphony keeps its own layer keyed to the Google event:
notes, a "free" flag, a discussion thread, related emails, travel time, and
per-day state when you skip it or mark it done. You can hide an event from
your view without touching the calendar.

**Passing.** An event does not expire, carry over, or need a decision. When
its day is gone it is gone from Today, and the calendar remembers it. Symphony's
notes on it remain, reachable from the notes stream and from the event itself
whenever you look back. Twelve months on, the event is a line in Google and
the notes are still yours.

## A routine

**Birth.** A routine is a rule, not a task, and it never becomes one. It is
born on the Routines page, from ⌘K when you add a recurrence ("every
Tuesday"), or from a recurring line on a paper plan. The rule says daily,
certain weekdays, monthly, yearly, "since I last did it", or the weekend.
Monday to Friday counts as every day. A collection is a routine with steps;
the parent carries the hour and the steps inherit it.

**Each morning.** Every day the same ladder decides whether it shows, and the
first rung that matches is the reason: resting, not today, hidden from the
timeline, another life area, not yours, a step inside a collection, or swept
by "hide daily routines". Timed routines sit in "Still ahead". Untimed ones
wait in the Today pin as "Available today" until you choose one, and then it
joins "My focus". A routine that has a rule but no time and no day rides on
the `/week` list asking to be dragged somewhere. Dropping it on a day rewrites
the rule; dropping it on a time writes a one-day exception so one drag cannot
move every future week.

**Each occurrence.** The first time you touch a routine on a given day,
Symphony creates that day's occurrence. Completing, skipping, deferring to
another day, and choosing it for today all write to that one occurrence. The
rule is never touched. A routine set to the weekend is one occurrence with a
two-day window, and a long weekend widens it by the federal days off, computed
rather than read from the holiday calendar. Doing it Saturday settles Sunday.
Skipping it Saturday does not, yet. That is an open question. Some routines
mint preparation tasks on their day, which are real timed tasks tied to the
occurrence.

**Resting.** A routine can rest until a date. Camp mornings rest until next
June. While it rests it shows nowhere, and it wakes by itself. On the Routines
page the year ribbon shows it in the month it returns.

**Through the year.** The Routines page reads Every day, Through the week,
Through the year, with the year rolling forward from this month so every
yearly routine appears once. Monthly ones gather in one pool. There are no
streaks and no scores anywhere. Retiring a routine means resting it with no
wake date or deleting the rule; the history of its occurrences stays.

## A note

**Birth.** A note is born in four places. You write one in the notes stream.
You jot one on a task, and the stream shows it titled by the task. You attach
one to an event. Or a Supernote page comes in, and the note carries the page
image with it. Long-form thinking does not come here at all; it lives in the
Obsidian vault, and vault notes are read-only if they ever appear. The stream
has no folders and no required title, because an operational scrap dies the
moment it must be named.

**Where it shows.** The stream runs newest first, grouped Today, Yesterday,
This week, Older, with a mark in the gutter for where it came from: written
here, on a task, from a page. Search finds it from ⌘K. A note on a task or an
event also shows on that item's panel, with markdown rendered.

**Life.** A note does not move and does not age. It has no rung, no date to
miss, and no decision to make. It follows the thing it is attached to: a
month task copied down to the week carries its note onto the copy. Privacy
comes from the note's own life area, and any attachment inherits the privacy
of what it hangs on.

**A year on.** The note is still in the stream, still searchable, and still
on the item it belonged to. There is no review of notes and no end-of-year
ritual for them. Nothing in Symphony ever asks what should become of a note.

## What the year exposes

- **Someday is a one-way door.** The route redirects to Today. Search and the
  assistant are the only way back.
- **Unstamped rows never leave "this period".** A month or season row that
  was never stamped with its period counts as the current one forever. In
  production, zero rows carry a month or season stamp.
- **The month count includes finished rows.** The week's month rail has no
  done filter.
- **Three writers skip the privacy label.** The capture edge function, the
  agent queue, and the MCP server land on the private default even when the
  intent was shared.
- **Skipping a weekend routine does not settle the window.** Only completing
  does. Decide whether a skip should.
- **Notes have no year-end.** That may be right. It is at least deliberate
  now that it is written down.
