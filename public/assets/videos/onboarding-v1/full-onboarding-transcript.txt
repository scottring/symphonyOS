Symphony helps you connect what matters in your life with what you need to do today. In Symphony, you can break a big goal into smaller goals, then choose tasks that help you achieve them.

Start with whatever’s on your mind. Press Command-K or Control-K, or tap the circle-plus button, to capture a thought, task, or idea in your Inbox. You don’t need to organize it right away. Later, decide whether it’s something to do, a goal to work toward, or information to keep—and add any details you’ll need.

Symphony gives you five places to plan. The year is for your bigger goals. The season and month help you decide what to focus on next. The week is where you choose your commitments, and today is where you get to work. You can start wherever you need to. Let’s follow one family goal through all five.

Suppose your goal for the year is to spend more quality time as a family.

For the season, you make that more specific: build a habit of spending time outdoors together. This month, you set a smaller goal: go on two family walks, and pick the two days you’d like to go.

To make the first walk happen, you add a task to this week’s plan: choose a trail for Saturday’s walk. When you’re ready to choose the trail, add that task to Today. Open it, compare the trail links you’ve saved alongside it, and choose one.

Of course, not everything starts with a bigger goal. Sometimes, something just needs your attention today. Suppose the pharmacy hasn’t filled a medication you need. You can add a task to Today: call the pharmacy about my missing medication.

You or Symphony’s AI can prepare the details beforehand: the correct store’s verified phone number, which medication is missing, and any notes from your last conversation. When you’re ready, open the task and tap the number to call. The details are right there. Afterward, record the answer and add any follow-up.

That takes care of the unexpected. For the jobs that come around regularly, routines help your family keep track of who’s responsible. Add taking out the trash as a Tuesday routine, then choose who will handle it.

Assign it to a particular family member, or leave it open for anyone to take care of. The assignment appears with the task, and when it’s marked done, everyone sees that it’s finished.

At the end of the week, take a few minutes to look back at what you finished and decide what to do with anything unfinished. If you still need to choose a trail for the family walk, you can carry that task into next week.

If researching a new tent can wait, save it in Someday. And if you’ve decided to keep an item you purchased, you can let go of the task to return it. Unfinished tasks don’t automatically become next week’s commitments. You choose what still deserves your time.
